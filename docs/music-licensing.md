# Music licensing

Stillroom distributes no Suno recordings. Its built-in presets are original
procedural compositions implemented in the MIT-licensed source. Selecting a local
folder does not change the rights in those recordings.

## Suno review — 11 October 2026

The [Suno terms](https://suno.com/terms), revised 10 August and effective
3 September 2026, distinguish paid-tier assignment from free-tier use. Commercial
use also depends on compliant output and an approved download within applicable
plan allocations. Remix output has separate noncommercial restrictions. The terms
do not guarantee copyright protection, and impose conditions concerning provenance
and unauthorized extraction. Assignment alone therefore does not establish that
every generated recording can be redistributed under unrestricted MIT terms.

Suno's [ownership guidance](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2416769) and
[commercial-use guidance](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2410177) further
distinguish the subscription held when a song was generated; a later upgrade does
not ordinarily grant retroactive commercial rights. These pages predate the
current terms, so the current contract must also be checked.

Our decision is narrower than a claim that Suno prohibits all public distribution:
we have not established an unrestricted redistribution grant for specific
recordings, so none are included. Users can choose music they are entitled to
play. A separate asset pack would require checking generation history, download
provenance, third-party rights and an asset-specific license rather than applying
the software's MIT license automatically.
