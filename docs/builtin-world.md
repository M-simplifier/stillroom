# Built-in music and scenery

Stillroom 0.11 opens into a complete listening room: soft piano, a still water
landscape, and an optional journey with environmental sound. Existing music
folders, pictures and preferences survive the update. Nothing is generated by
an online service while the app runs.

## A score that continues

The four moods are Soft piano, Warm keys, Quiet pulse and Wood & air. Each has
an authored vocabulary of harmony, rhythm, dynamics and spacing. The score
chooses small variations within it, rather than concatenating unrelated songs.
Next changes the mood explicitly. Imported recordings retain shuffle and
finite-track crossfades.

`Audio.Composition` queries events near an Integer sample position. Rational
beats keep timing independent of callback sizes. A 24-second lookback includes
all possible sounding tails; loading rejects longer instruments. Query cost
depends on a bounded nearby window, not how long the app has been open.
`Audio.Mix` uses validated immutable banks, deterministic voice order and Double
accumulation before Float output. The same requested PCM interval is invariant
under block partition in the tested build.

`Runtime.Audio` supplies blocks on a bound thread and owns device lifetime.
Control operations and the three streams are serialized. Music keeps its
position through a break; it eases to 40% while journey sound is active, then
returns. This is application headroom, not a measurement of sound at the ear.

## Sound belongs to the place

`Audio.Environment` takes four observations: aboard/on foot, speed, forest
weight and shore distance. Wind and foliage vary slowly; water fades with
distance. Wheel phase integrates speed instead of multiplying current speed
by absolute time, which would jump when speed changes. Small body sounds occur
by distance travelled. State and simultaneous voices are bounded. Leaving the
journey fades to silence and freezes its audio phases. Pause and master volume
apply to music and ambience together.

The prototype comparison favoured audible wheel presence. The accepted setting
uses a car mix of 0.90; there are no sharp sleeper clicks, birdsong cues or engine
rumble. This is a design choice, not a physiological result.

## Rebuild the assets

The [audio tools](../tools/audio/README.md) regenerate 62 instrument samples and
seven environmental samples. All 69 matched the shipped WAVs byte for byte in
the documented environment. The piano derives from credited Salamander Grand
Piano recordings under CC BY 3.0; other sounds are code-generated under MIT.
The app mixes resident PCM and needs no Python or additional downloads.

The [wallpaper tool](../tools/wallpapers/README.md) renders four static landscapes
from authored shapes, colors and seeds. The output is bundled to avoid rendering
cost during work. The six earlier wallpapers remain optional choices.

## Evidence and limits

Independent waveform and speed/phase oracles complement partition, validation,
seed and migration tests. Native muted tests exercise pause, finite crossfades,
continuous sources, drawing stalls, stream cleanup and failure paths. A 4096-frame
environment block took about 0.92 ms on the development machine, against 92.88 ms
of audio. This excludes device timing and does not guarantee every deadline.
Loading all instrument banks used approximately 288 MB of maximum residency in
the isolated source test. Long real-world listening, other devices and power
cost remain unmeasured.

Auditions informed the musical choices: repeated block chords were replaced by
separate voices; adding a bare beat beneath the piano was rejected. These are
individual preferences, not evidence of improved attention. Research on
[self-selected music](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-60218-z) and
[music with lyrics](https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.273) is conditional on listeners
and tasks; it does not establish a universal preset or treatment. Time-window
composition has a useful precedent in
[Tidal's pattern model](https://doi.org/10.1145/2633638.2633647).
